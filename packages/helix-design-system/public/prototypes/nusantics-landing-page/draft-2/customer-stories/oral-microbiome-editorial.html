Microbiome research

Mapping the oral microbiome from a simple cheek swab

A university lab profiled the bacteria in 120 volunteers' mouths using BiomeSeq and the Nusantics Dashboard, without building a sequencing pipeline of their own.

Oral Microbiome Research Lab • Bandung, Indonesia • November 16, 2026

HomeKnowledge HubCustomer StoriesMapping the oral microbiome from a simple cheek swab

At the Oral Microbiome Research Lab, a team of biologists profiled the bacteria in 120 volunteers' mouths using BiomeSeq and the Nusantics Dashboard, without building a sequencing pipeline of their own.

A question that starts in the mouth

The mouth holds hundreds of bacterial species. Changes in that community have been linked to gum disease, cavities and, in some studies, wider health. The team wanted to see how it differs between volunteers of different ages and diets.

A study like that needs many samples from many people. Blood draws were out of the question. A cheek swab takes seconds, needs no needle and can be done at a community clinic.

Collecting at scale from a cheek swab

Volunteers rubbed a sterile swab against the inside of the cheek for about 30 seconds. The tip went into a tube of stabilising buffer, which keeps the bacterial DNA intact until the sample reaches the lab.

From swab to a sequencing-ready library

In the lab, the team extracted DNA from each swab, then used BiomeSeq to amplify the V3–V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene, clean up the product, add indexes and normalise the library. The PCR step uses a ready-to-use mix in a “Thaw-Mix-Run” format, so there is less pipetting and fewer mixing errors across a 96-sample plate. Libraries ran on an Illumina MiSeq system.

From reads to insight, without a pipeline of their own

The lab has biologists, not bioinformaticians. Each BiomeSeq bundle includes a unique code for the Nusantics Dashboard. The team uploaded their sequencing data and received taxonomy profiles for every sample, with relative abundance charts they could filter by age group and diet.
